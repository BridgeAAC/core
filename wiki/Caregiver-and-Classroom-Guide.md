# Caregiver & Classroom Guide

This guide is designed for parents, Speech-Language Pathologists (SLPs), Special Education teachers, occupational therapists, and paraprofessionals working with communicators who use BridgeAAC.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Autonomy & Dignity First**: Communication is an inherent human right. An AAC device should be treated as the user's voice — never removed as a punishment, never withheld, and never manipulated by forcing a communicator's physical hands.

---

## 1. The Core Principle: Aided Language Stimulation (Modeling)

Research consistently demonstrates that individuals learn to use AAC the same way infants learn spoken language: by **seeing others use it repeatedly in natural daily contexts**. This is known as **Aided Language Stimulation** (or "Modeling").

### How to Model with BridgeAAC:
1. **Talk AND Tap**: Speak naturally with your voice, and simultaneously tap one or two key words on the BridgeAAC board.
   - *Example*: You say: *"Do you want to **go** to the **park**?"* You tap: `GO` &rarr; `PLACES` &rarr; `PARK`.
2. **Model One Step Above**: Model language at or slightly above the communicator's current expressive level.
   - If the communicator uses 1-word taps (`juice`), model 2-word combinations (`more juice`, `want juice`).
   - If the communicator uses 2-word combinations, model 3-word combinations (`I want juice`).
3. **Never Force Hands ("Hand-Under-Hand" vs "Hand-Over-Hand")**: Never physically grab a communicator's hand or force their finger onto a button. Hand-over-hand pressure can cause tactile defensiveness and prompt dependence. Instead, model the word on the screen where they can observe.
4. **Respond to All Communication**: Even if a tap seems accidental, treat it as meaningful communication. If the user taps `STOP`, stop what you are doing and say: *"You said STOP, so I stopped."* This teaches the power of cause and effect.

## 2. Classroom Device Management & Locking

To ensure the device remains a true communication tool rather than a toy, keep BridgeAAC locked to the screen during instructional time:

### Apple iPad: Setup Guided Access
1. Open iPad **Settings** &rarr; **Accessibility** &rarr; **Guided Access**.
2. Turn **Guided Access** ON and set a passcode known only to school staff and parents.
3. Open BridgeAAC.
4. **Triple-click the top button / Home button** to activate.
5. In Guided Access options, disable the hardware volume and sleep buttons if necessary.
6. The student cannot exit the app, switch to web browsers, or access other apps.

### BridgeAAC App PIN Protection
1. In BridgeAAC, tap **Settings** &rarr; **System**.
2. Under **Security & PIN Lock**, set a 4-digit PIN.
3. Enable **Protect Edit Mode**, **Protect Vocabularies**, and **Protect Settings**.
4. The student can communicate freely, but tapping the Edit pencil or Settings gear prompts for the PIN.

## 3. Creating Custom Lesson & Routine Boards

Classrooms often have recurring activities (Morning Circle, Cooking Class, Science Lab, Recess):

1. Enter **Edit Mode** (enter PIN).
2. Tap **Page Settings** &rarr; **New Page**.
3. Name the page (e.g., `Circle Time`).
4. Choose a grid size (e.g., `4x4` or `5x5`).
5. Add activity-specific core and fringe words:
   - *Social*: `good morning`, `my turn`, `your turn`.
   - *Activities*: `calendar`, `weather`, `story`, `song`.
   - *Questions*: `who`, `what`, `where`.
6. Add a **Back** button and a **Home** button in the top corners for easy navigation.
7. Link to this new page from the main `Places` or `School` folder on the Home page.

## 4. Backup & Disaster Recovery

AAC devices are prone to being dropped, submerged in water, or lost. **Never let weeks of custom vocabulary work exist on only one device.**

### Weekly Backup Routine:
1. Tap **Vocab** in the top navigation bar.
2. Tap **Export** &rarr; **Export as BridgeAAC (`.bridge`)** or **Export as TouchChat (`.ce`)**.
3. Save the file with a date stamp (e.g., `BridgeAAC_Backup_Emily_2026-11-09.bridge`).
4. Upload the file to Google Drive, iCloud, or email it to the therapist.
5. If the iPad is broken or replaced, open BridgeAAC on the new device, tap **Vocab** &rarr; **Import File**, and restore the entire vocabulary in seconds.

---

## 5. Low-Tech Paper Backup

Technology can fail, batteries run out, and devices cannot go into swimming pools or bathtubs. Every AAC user should have a **low-tech printed paper backup**:

### How to Print Your Board:
1. Open BridgeAAC on a tablet or desktop computer.
2. Navigate to your Home page (or any critical sub-page).
3. Use your browser's Print feature (`Ctrl+P` on Windows/Linux, `Cmd+P` on macOS, or Share &rarr; Print on iPad).
4. Select **Landscape** orientation and ensure background graphics are enabled.
5. Print the page, laminate it with plastic, and store it in the communicator's backpack or wheelchair pouch.
